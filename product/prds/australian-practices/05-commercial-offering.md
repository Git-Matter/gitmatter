# PRD 05: A clear sole-practice commercial offering

## Problem and outcome

A sole practitioner needs to understand what they are buying, what setup requires, and which AI costs are separate. Open-source installation instructions do not answer those buying questions.

## First release

Create an Australian sole-practice offering page reachable from the public navigation/homepage. Explain the guided workflows, assisted setup, existing self-hosting option, and an AUD pilot quote path. Do not invent a published monthly fee or implement billing until product ownership selects the price and terms.

## Requirements

1. Describe concrete deliverables: checked matter briefing, agreement review, draft client update, Word handoff, and attributed history.
2. Explain that AI subscription/API usage may be separately payable to the selected provider and depends on the execution path.
3. Say prices will be quoted in AUD and the quote will state whether GST applies and is included. Do not claim a GST registration or tax treatment that has not been established.
4. If no authorised fee exists, label the commercial path **Request an AUD pilot quote**. It must not show $0, a fabricated saving, a fake discount, or an implied free hosted plan.
5. Keep the MIT/self-hosted option accurate and distinct from hosted onboarding and support.
6. Use established contact destinations. Opening an enquiry draft must not send it or imply acceptance into the pilot.

## Acceptance

- A visitor can find the offering, understand separate provider costs, and open an editable quote request.
- Public page has a meaningful title, description, canonical URL, and appropriate navigation links.
- No payment is charged, contract accepted, or subscription created by the initial implementation.
- Copy contains no unverified guarantees about outcomes, data location, or certifications.

## Measurement and limits

Track qualified enquiries and paid continuation once real pilots occur. Price discovery is an experiment, not a claim of validated demand. A checkout, metered billing, cancellation automation, and a final service agreement depend on commercial decisions and are deferred explicitly.
